BASIC + CLINICAL PEARL
What is the mechanism of action of Atropine in treating symptomatic sinus bradycardia?
It acts as a competitive antagonist at muscarinic acetylcholine receptors ($M_2$), blocking parasympathetic vagal input to the SA and AV nodes, thereby increasing heart rate and conduction velocity.
High-Yield Clinical Pearl
Low doses of atropine (< 0.5 mg) can paradoxically cause transient bradycardia due to central vagal stimulation or blockade of presynaptic inhibitory $M_1$ autoreceptors.
Don't Confuse / Exam Trap
Atropine is completely ineffective in heart transplant patients because the transplanted donor heart is surgically denervated!

Acute Chest Pain Radiating to Left Jaw in a 58-Year-Old Male

A 58-year-old male with a 20-pack-year smoking history and poorly controlled hypertension presents to the Emergency Department with severe, crushing retrosternal chest pain that began 45 minutes ago while shoveling snow. He is diaphoretic, pale, and nauseous. Physical examination reveals bilateral basilar lung crackles and an $S_4$ gallop on cardiac auscultation.
INITIAL ECG STRIP:
ST-segment elevations of 3.5 mm in leads V1-V4 with reciprocal ST depressions in II, III, and aVF.
CLINICAL DECISION STEP 1 / 3

Which coronary artery is most likely occluded, and what is the immediate definitive management?

✓ Correct Clinical Formulation
ST elevations across V1-V4 represent an Anterior Wall STEMI, supplied by the LAD artery. Because the patient is within the 12-hour window and hypotensive with pulmonary edema (Killip Class II), immediate reperfusion via primary PCI within 90 minutes is the life-saving standard.

Question 4 of 25 Pharmacology
A 64-year-old female with chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF, EF 28%) and hypertension is initiated on a new medication. Two weeks later, routine labs reveal serum potassium of 5.3 mEq/L (baseline 4.4) and serum creatinine increase from 1.0 to 1.2 mg/dL. She also reports a persistent dry nocturnal cough. Which of the following is the direct mechanism causing her respiratory symptom?
A
Inhibition of angiotensin II type-1 ($AT_1$) receptors in alveolar capillaries
B
Decreased degradation of Bradykinin and Substance P due to ACE inhibition
C
Blockade of aldosterone receptors in bronchial smooth muscle
D
Direct vagal nerve stimulation by increased renin secretion
Answer Explanation (Option B)

Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE / Kininase II) is responsible for breaking down bradykinin and substance P. ACE inhibitors prevent this inactivation, leading to pulmonary accumulation of bradykinin, which stimulates unmyelinated C-fibers in the airway and triggers the classic dry cough.
