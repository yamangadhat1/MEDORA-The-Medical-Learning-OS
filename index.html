Comprehensive Educational Objective

Angiotensin II normally causes potent efferent arteriolar constriction, raising intraglomerular capillary pressure to maintain GFR at the cost of hyperfiltration stress. ACE inhibitors block Angiotensin II, causing efferent vasodilation, which lowers intraglomerular pressure and reduces microalbuminuria.

Why other options are incorrect:
• Option A (Afferent constriction): Caused by NSAIDs (via prostaglandin inhibition), which drops GFR and can induce acute kidney injury.
• Option C: Increased Bowman hydrostatic pressure occurs in urinary tract obstruction (hydronephrosis).

What is the first-line pharmacologic antidote for organophosphate / sarin toxicity, and what is its mechanism?
Atropine (competitive muscarinic antagonist to control secretions and bronchoconstriction) followed by Pralidoxime (2-PAM) to regenerate acetylcholinesterase before aging occurs.
Clinical Pearl: Atropine only relieves muscarinic signs (DUMBBELSS). Pralidoxime is required to reverse nicotinic muscle paralysis!
